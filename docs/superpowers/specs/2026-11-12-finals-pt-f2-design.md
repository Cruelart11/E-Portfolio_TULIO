### Description

This exercise continues the chunking experiment by comparing twelve configurations for a FAISS-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation system using the 2024 Workers' Statutory Monetary Benefits Handbook. I tested chunk sizes of 300, 500, 700, and 1,000 characters, each with zero, 10%, and 20% overlap, using the same nine worker-focused questions across easy, average, and difficult levels. Retrieval performance was measured using Precision@3, Recall@3, and MRR@3, while the number of generated chunks and indexing time were also recorded. The results showed that C12, with a chunk size of 1,000 and an overlap of 200, provided the strongest overall balance, reaching 0.556 Precision@3, 0.944 Recall@3, and 0.685 MRR@3.

### Learning Reflection

This activity helped me understand why chunk settings should be selected through evidence rather than assumption. Smaller chunks sometimes captured specific details, but they could separate rates from the conditions or computations that belonged with them. Larger chunks preserved more useful context, although they also introduced trade-offs in precision and efficiency. Comparing C10 and C12 was especially meaningful because C10 produced fewer chunks and ranked the first relevant result slightly earlier, while C12 recovered more of the evidence needed for difficult questions. I learned that choosing the best configuration requires examining several metrics together and considering what matters most for the document. For labor-benefit information, I found that complete and dependable evidence was more important than a small reduction in indexing work.
